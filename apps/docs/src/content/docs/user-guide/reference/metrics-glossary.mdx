title: Methodology
description: Exact definitions of every figure Tradr computes — gross and net P&L, return percentage, both risk/reward ratios, win rate, profit factor, expectancy, and how the breakdowns and calendar group your closed positions.
---

Every number in Tradr is derived from your fills. Nothing is stored as a running
total, so correcting a fill corrects every figure that depends on it.

This page gives the exact definition of each one, including the parts that are
easy to assume wrongly.

## Per position

### Average entry / average exit

The quantity-weighted average price across the fills of that type.

Two entries — 100 at 190.00 and 50 at 195.00 — average to **191.6667**, not
192.50. The larger fill counts for more.

### Gross P&L

```
(average exit − average entry) × quantity exited × direction × contract size
```

Direction is +1 for a long and −1 for a short. Contract size is 100 for an
option and 1 for a stock. Fees are not included.

### Net P&L

Gross P&L, minus the fees attributable to the quantity exited. **This is the
figure that reaches your account balance.**

### Fees

Not simply "the fees on the exit". Each exit carries:

- its own fee, plus
- a **proportional share of the entry fees**, for the quantity being closed.

Close 60 of 150 units and 60/150 of the entry fees come with it. Close the
remaining 90 and the rest follows. This keeps a partial exit from either escaping
its share of the entry cost or paying all of it up front.

The displayed fee figure is derived as gross minus net, so
`gross − fees = net` holds exactly rather than to within a rounding error.

### Return %

```
net P&L ÷ (average entry × quantity exited × contract size + allocated entry fees) × 100
```

The denominator is what the closed portion cost you. For a short position it is
the proceeds of the sale, not the margin you posted — margin varies by broker and
Tradr does not know yours.

### Target R/R

```
|target price − average entry| ÷ |average entry − stop loss|
```

Planned reward per unit of risk, from the trade plan you recorded.

**It moves when you scale in.** Both distances are measured from the _average_
entry, so a second fill at a worse price re-bases the ratio even though your
target and stop have not moved.

Null when there is no target, no stop, or when the stop equals the average entry
— the risk per unit would be zero.

### Actual R/R

```
(average exit − average entry) × direction ÷ |average entry − stop loss|
```

What you actually got, in the same units as the plan. **Signed** — negative when
the trade went against you, so a −1.0 means you lost exactly the risk you planned.

## Across positions

These appear on the performance page, one currency at a time. Every figure below
counts **closed positions only**. The exception is Total net P&L: it follows your
fills, so a partial exit counts before its position is flat. The dashboard's
Statistics summary widget shows several of these same figures.

### Total positions

Every flat position the range brings into view, counted once, one currency at a
time. A position comes into view once it has gone flat, meaning it reached zero
open quantity. That includes a position that went flat inside the range, and one
that had a fill inside the range but went flat outside it. When none are in range
the count reads 0, never blank.

### Total net P&L

The sum of the realised net P&L of every fill in the range, one currency at a
time. It comes straight from the fills, so a partial exit counts before its
position is flat. That makes it equal the last point of the equity curve. Its
population is every fill in range, wider than the count above: a position can add
to this total before it is counted. With no fills in range it reads 0, never blank.

### Win, loss, breakeven

A position is classified by its net P&L **rounded to the currency's smallest
unit**. Exactly zero after rounding is a **breakeven**, not a win and not a loss.
A position that made half a cent is a breakeven in USD.

### Win rate

```
wins ÷ (wins + losses) × 100
```

**Breakevens are excluded from the denominator**, not counted as losses. A
record of 6 wins, 3 losses and 1 breakeven gives a 66.7% win rate, not 60%.

That choice makes the figure comparable between a strategy that scratches trades
and one that does not. Breakevens are reported separately as the breakeven rate,
which _does_ divide by the total.

Null when nothing has been decided — no wins and no losses in range.

### Breakeven rate

```
breakevens ÷ total positions × 100
```

Unlike win rate, this one uses every closed position as its denominator.

### Average win / average loss

The mean net P&L of winning and of losing positions respectively. Average loss is
negative. Breakevens are in neither.

### Profit factor

```
sum of winning net P&L ÷ |sum of losing net P&L|
```

Above 1.0 means the wins outweigh the losses. Null when there are **no wins or no
losses** — not infinity, and not zero. With no losses there is no meaningful
ratio, and reporting one would flatter a two-trade record.

### Largest win / largest loss

The single best and single worst net P&L in range.

### Expectancy

```
sum of net P&L over closed positions ÷ total positions
```

The average net P&L a position returns. Unlike win rate, **the denominator is
the total**, not the decided trades — so every breakeven is counted, each one
pulling the figure toward zero.

The same figure follows from the averages:

```
(wins ÷ total) × average win + (losses ÷ total) × average loss
```

The weights divide by the total, so a run of scratches lowers your expectancy
even though a breakeven adds nothing to the numerator.

Null when nothing has closed in range.

### Sharpe ratio

Tradr does not report a Sharpe ratio.

A Sharpe ratio is built from a series of period returns measured against a
capital base, and Tradr keeps neither. Return is measured per position, against
what the closed portion cost — see Return % above — not against your account
over time. Balances are current, not historical, so there is no record of what
the account was worth period by period to take a mean and a standard deviation
from.

Reporting one would first need a capital-base series — your account value over
time — with deposits and withdrawals separated out, so that funding the account
is not read as a gain.

### Equity curve

The running total of net P&L, bucket by bucket, over the range you selected. It
starts at zero rather than at your account balance: it shows what your trading
did, not what your account is worth.

Bucket boundaries come from your reporting timezone, described below.

## Breakdowns

A breakdown groups your **closed positions that went flat inside the range**, one
currency at a time, and reports the statistics above for each group.

The population is narrower than the statistics above. Only positions that went
flat — reached zero open quantity — at an instant inside the selected range are
grouped. A position that merely had a fill in the range, but went flat outside
it, is left out. Each currency is grouped on its own.

### By symbol

One row per instrument. An **option groups under its underlying**, so every
contract on the same stock shares one row; a stock groups under its own symbol.

### By weekday and by hour

Both read **the moment the position went flat**, expressed in your reporting
timezone — the same instant that places the position on the calendar. A position
that went flat late on a Friday evening is a Friday, and stays a Friday wherever
you open Tradr.

### Rows that add up, and rows that overlap

By symbol, by weekday and by hour, **each position lands in exactly one row**, so
the rows add up to the range total.

By tag they do not. A position is **counted once under each of its tags**, so a
position with two tags appears in two rows and the rows can total more than the
range. An **Untagged** row holds every position with no tag, so nothing is
missing even though the rows no longer sum to the total.

### Why the total can differ from the Statistics panel

A breakdown's total is the **closed-in-range** total: only the positions that
went flat inside the range. The Statistics panel is wider — it counts every flat
position the range brings into view, including ones that had a fill inside the
range but went flat outside it. So the breakdown's total P&L, its position count
and its rates can all read differently from the panel; they are answering
different questions.

## Calendar

Each day cell carries two figures from two different events.

**The P&L is the realised P&L of the fills that landed that day** — the same rule
as the daily series and the equity curve above. A partial exit counts on the day
it filled, so a cell can show a P&L for a position that has not closed.

**The position count is the number of positions that went flat that day.** A
position whose fills span more than one day adds its realised P&L to each of
those days, but is counted once, on the day it went flat.

Days come from your reporting timezone, the same zone the rest of this page uses.

A **week row's total covers only the days of the shown month** — the trailing and
leading days that belong to a neighbouring month are not added in. The **month
total** equals that month's bucket on the performance chart: the same daily P&L,
summed a different way.

## Elsewhere in the app

These figures appear away from the performance and position views, on the
account, calculator, fee, tax and options screens.

### Account balance

An account's balance is its **starting balance plus every ledger credit, minus
every ledger debit**. Only seven ledger entry types move it. They are realised
position P&L and its reversal, balance adjustments, deposits and withdrawals,
and the reversals of those deposits and withdrawals.

Nothing here reads a market quote, so the balance is a cash figure valued at
cost. It divides into two halves. **Cash** is the free part. **Position value**
is the capital tied up in open positions, at what you paid — or, for a short,
what you received. Cash is the balance minus that position value, so the halves
always sum back to the balance.

Position value counts open positions only; a closed one has returned its capital
to cash. Both halves are in one currency, the account's own.

### Trade calculator

The calculator turns a risk budget into a position size. **Per-unit risk** is
the distance from entry to stop, `|entry − stop|`. **Contract size** is 100 for
an options trade and 1 for a stock.

The **risk-derived size** is the risk budget divided by
`per-unit risk × contract size`, floored to whole units. The budget is a dollar
amount, or a percentage of your account balance. A **buying-power cap** can
lower it: buying power divided by `entry × contract size`, floored. The smaller
size wins.

From the final size come the **dollar risk**, `per-unit risk × size × contract
size`, and the **total position value**, `entry × size × contract size`.
**Risk/reward** is `|target − entry| ÷ |entry − stop|`, shown only when you set
a target. **Estimated fees and the breakeven price** come from the journal's own
fee rules, through `calculateFees`.

Every figure stays in the currency of the prices you type; there is no
conversion. When the calculator cannot size a trade it returns a status instead:

- **nothing-to-size-against** — the balance is zero or negative, so a percentage
  of it risks nothing.
- **exceeds-maximum** — the derived dollar risk is past the built-in ceiling.
- **buying-power-zero** — the buying power will not fund a single unit.

### Fee rollup

The fee rollup totals the fees you paid over a year. Each account gets a
**stock-fee total and an options-fee total**. The two add to that account's
overall total, in the account's own currency.

Above the accounts, the totals are summed **per currency**. Those per-currency
sums then convert once, at the year-end spot rate, into a single **grand total
in your display currency**. A year with no fills reports empty totals and no
grand total, never a zero.

### Tax summary

The tax summary reports your **realised P&L for the year, one figure per
currency**. What else it shows depends on the tax jurisdiction set in your
profile.

**Only a US jurisdiction splits that P&L into short-term and long-term.** A
position held 365 days or fewer is short-term; a longer hold is long-term.
Canada and every other jurisdiction leave the split empty.

Losses can raise a flag. A **US wash sale** or a **Canadian superficial loss**
is flagged when a matching trade sits within 30 days either side of the loss.
The flag is a note only — **Tradr never adjusts your cost basis** — and
jurisdiction "other" raises no flags.

### Black-Scholes

The options tools include a Black-Scholes pricer. It takes six inputs: the spot
price `S`, strike `K`, time to expiry in years `T`, and volatility `σ`. It also
takes the risk-free rate `r` and an optional dividend yield `q`, for a call or a
put.

It returns the option **price** and five Greeks. These are **delta**, **gamma**,
**theta per day**, **vega per 1%**, and **rho per 1%**. Theta is scaled to one
day, and vega and rho to a one-point move — a 1% change in volatility or in the
rate.

The four inputs `S`, `K`, `T` and `σ` must each be positive. A zero or negative
one stops the model, so no number comes back. When volatility and time are so
small that `σ × √T` drops below a set threshold, the pricer returns the limit
value: the discounted intrinsic payoff. There the standard formula would divide
by that near-zero quantity, so the analytic limit is used instead. The price is
in the currency of the spot and strike you enter.

## Things worth knowing

**Realized P&L does not wait for a close.** A partial exit realizes its share
immediately, so an open position can carry a realized P&L and contribute to your
balance. The performance statistics above are a different question — they count
positions that have gone flat, which is why a half-closed position appears in your
balance but not yet in your win rate.

**Day, week and month come from your reporting timezone.** Tradr buckets P&L in
the zone stored against your user, not the zone of the browser you are reading
from. A fill late in the evening lands in the same bucket wherever you open
Tradr. Set the zone under **Settings → Profile**.

That zone is not an account's trading-day timezone, and neither is derived from
the other. The trading-day timezone belongs to one account and decides whether a
position can be re-entered the same day. The reporting timezone belongs to you
and decides how these figures are cut.

**Currencies are never mixed.** Statistics are computed per currency. Positions in
a currency Tradr has no rate for are excluded and reported as excluded rather than
silently dropped.

**A reopened position keeps its last flat figures** until it goes flat again.
Recomputing from the current fills would give a different number, because the
fills have changed since — so the last completed result is held rather than
recalculated.
