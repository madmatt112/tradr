This is a hand-held first session. By the end you will have a brokerage account
(an **account** in Tradr) and one position taken from draft to closed. You will
also have read your own figures back. It takes about ten minutes, and every step
tells you what you should see.

Tradr walks you through the same four steps inside the app. Choose **Walk me
through it** on your first dashboard and the product guides you through them in
order. This page is that same route, written down.

This guide describes the **hosted app at `app.tradr.cloud`**. If you run your own
instance, the screens are identical. The few differences are called out in
**Self-hosted** notes.

  Open your instance at its own address instead (by default
  `http://localhost:8080`). See [Self-host with Docker
  Compose](/self-hosting/docker-compose/) to stand one up first.
</SelfHosted>

## Before you start

You need:

- An email address and a browser. Use the latest Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge.
- Nothing else. Tradr works as a plain manual journal. This tutorial needs no
  broker connection, no API keys, and no payment.

## Step 1 — Register

1. Open **https://app.tradr.cloud**.
2. Choose **Register**.
3. Enter your email and a password, then confirm the password.
4. Choose **Register** again.

**Expected result:** you land on a setup screen headed **Welcome to Tradr**. It is
not an empty dashboard. Below it sits a **Get set up** checklist of four items.

<Screenshot name="dashboard-first-login" alt="The Tradr dashboard on first login. A Welcome to Tradr card offers Create my first account, Walk me through it, and Add sample data, above a Get set up checklist of four items." />

The welcome card offers three ways forward:

- **Create my first account** — the path this guide follows.
- **Walk me through it** — the same four steps, guided inside the app.
- **Add sample data** — a ready-made account to look around in.

## Step 2 — Create a brokerage account

Every position, fill and ledger entry is booked against an account. Nothing else
in Tradr works until one exists.

A Tradr account mirrors a real brokerage account: the same currency, the same
starting balance, the same trades. It is not connected to your broker. Tradr
never places or executes trades. You record trades you have already made.

1. Choose **Create my first account**.
2. Fill in the form described below.
3. Choose **Create**.

<Screenshot name="new-account-dialog" alt="The New Account dialog, showing its six fields: Name, Currency, Trading-day timezone, Starting balance, Default risk %, and Brokerage." />

The form has six fields, in this order.

**Name.** Name the account after the brokerage account it mirrors. You pick it by
this name every time you log a position.

**Currency.** The currency this account trades in. Its balance, fees and P&L are
all recorded and shown in this currency.

**Trading-day timezone.** This defaults to `America/New_York` because NYSE,
NASDAQ and NYSE Arca all operate on US Eastern. It defines the trading day for
this account. That is what decides whether a position can be re-entered the same
day. It is not your reporting timezone — see [the next
section](#your-reporting-timezone-is-a-separate-setting).

**Starting balance.** The account's opening cash, and the baseline every later
figure is measured against. Set it once, here. The balance Tradr shows you is
this figure plus every ledger entry. Editing it later would move every historical
balance with it, so **it cannot be changed once the account exists**. Leave it
empty and the account starts at zero.

**Default risk %.** The share of this account's balance you risk on a single
trade. It prefills the trade calculator, and you can override it on any one
calculation without changing the account. Pick **1%**, **2%** or **3%** — each
one shows what ten losing trades in a row would cost, and **2%** is chosen for
you. **No rule** sets none, and leaves the calculator's risk percent empty.
Unlike the starting balance, you can change this whenever you want.

**Brokerage.** Choose a brokerage and Tradr records this account's fees from that
brokerage's fee schedule. Leaving it on **None** is valid. You then enter fees
yourself on each fill, and you can attach a brokerage later.

**Expected result:** your dashboard replaces the welcome screen. The first
checklist item ticks itself.

Open **Accounts** to see what you made. The list shows **Name**, **Currency**,
and an unlabelled last column holding a **⋯** menu with **Edit** and **Delete**
in it. Balances are not in this list. They appear in the dashboard's **Account
Balances** widget.

<Screenshot name="accounts-list" alt="The Accounts page, listing one account under a Name column and a Currency column, with an unlabelled third column holding a row menu. A banner above it marks the figures on screen as sample data." />

The figure above carries the sample account rather than one of your own, so the
sample-data banner sits above it.

Tradr supports **multiple accounts in different currencies** and consolidates
them into a single view. One account is enough for this tutorial. See [Accounts &
the multi-currency ledger](/user-guide/accounts/) when you are ready to add more.

### Your reporting timezone is a separate setting

Separately from the account, Tradr stores one **reporting timezone** for you. It
is the zone your P&L is bucketed into by day, week and month. It follows you, so
those figures stay the same wherever you open Tradr.

Tradr stored one from your browser when you registered. Confirm or correct it
under **Settings → Profile**.

Setting one zone never sets the other. Changing your reporting timezone leaves
every account's trading-day timezone untouched. Changing an account's leaves your
reporting timezone untouched.

| | Trading-day timezone | Reporting timezone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Belongs to | One account | You |
| Set under | The account form | Settings → Profile |
| Decides | Whether a position can be re-entered the same day | How your P&L is bucketed by day, week and month |
| Starts as | `America/New_York` | The zone your browser reported at registration |

### Prefer to look around first?

Choose **Add sample data** on the welcome screen. Tradr creates one sample
account with a fixed set of positions in it. A banner then marks every figure on
screen as sample data.

Sample data and your own accounts cannot both exist. Choose **Remove sample
data** in the banner when you are ready to enter your own. No checklist item
completes on sample data alone.

## Step 3 — Size a trade in the calculator

Open **Calculator**. There is nothing to submit, the results recompute as you
type, and no trade is created. Neither your entries nor the figures they produce
are stored.

Tradr records one fact here, the first time a calculation succeeds: a timestamp.
That is how the **Get set up** checklist can tell this step is done. Nothing
about the calculation itself is kept.

1. Enter the **Entry price**.
2. Enter the **Stop loss**. The distance from entry to stop is your per-unit risk.
3. Enter a **Target price (optional)** if you have one.
4. Under **Risk**, choose **Dollar** or **Percent**. Dollar is the default.
5. Choose the **Account** you are trading.
6. Set the amount you are willing to lose.

**Direction** starts on **Long** and **Mode** on **Stock**, so a long stock trade
needs neither. **Symbol** changes no figure. With a quote provider configured, a
symbol unlocks **Pull last price**, which fills the entry price for you.

Under **Percent**, **Risk percent** prefills from that account's **Default risk
%** when it has one. Under **Dollar**, you type the **Dollar risk** yourself.
Either way the figure applies to this calculation only. The account keeps its own
rule.

The account does two more things. It supplies the currency the results are shown
in. It also caps the position at the buying power the account has.

**Expected result:** a **Position Sizing** card gives you the **Position size**,
the **Per-unit risk**, the **Actual dollar risk**, and the **Total position
value**. With a target, a **Risk / Reward** card adds the **Per-unit reward** and
the **Risk/Reward ratio**.

<Screenshot name="calculator" alt="The Trade Calculator, with entry 182.40, stop 178.00 and target 191.20 on the left, and a Position Sizing card reading 113 units on the right beside a Risk / Reward card." />

The figure above risks $500 at the prices the worked AAPL trade below uses. Entry
182.40 against a stop at 178.00 is $4.40 of risk per share, which buys 113 shares
worth $20,611.20. The target at 191.20 pays $8.80 a share, a reward of twice the
risk.

Position size is rounded down to whole units. That is why the actual dollar risk
lands at or under your budget rather than on it. Here it is $497.20, not the
$500 you asked for.

See [Use the trade calculator](/user-guide/trade-calculator/) for fee estimates
and the options mode.

## Step 4 — Log the position

A position in Tradr is one trade and every fill that belongs to it. It moves
through three states.

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | A plan you have not entered. It posts nothing to your ledger and leaves your balance where it was. |
| **Open** | You are in the trade. Its cost is held against the account as position value. |
| **Closed** | You have exited in full. |

1. Open **Positions**.
2. Choose **New Position**.
3. Enter the **Symbol** and choose the **Side**, the **Asset Type**, and the **Account**.
4. Add **Notes** describing the setup. Why you took the trade is the part you will
   want back later.
5. Choose **Create**.

That is the whole form for a stock. There is no quantity and no price here —
both come from fills. Setting **Asset Type** to **Option** replaces **Symbol**
with the contract's underlying, expiry, type, and strike.

**Expected result:** the position is created as a **draft**. Your account balance
is unchanged, because a draft is a plan rather than a trade.

### Record the entry fill

1. Choose **Add Fill**.
2. Leave **Type** on **Entry**.
3. Enter the **Price**, the **Quantity**, any **Fees**, and the **Date & Time**.
4. Choose **Add**.

### Open the position

Choose **Open Position**.

The button stays disabled until an entry fill exists, and its tooltip then reads
*Add an entry fill first*.

**Expected result:** the position is tagged **open**. Its cost is now held
against the account as position value.

## Step 5 — Close it and see the stats

1. Choose **Add Fill**.
2. Set **Type** to **Exit**.
3. Enter the exit **Price**, the **Quantity**, any **Fees**, and the **Date & Time**.
4. Choose **Add**.

Partial exits are ordinary. Add one fill per exit, and Tradr averages them for
you.

**Realized P&L does not wait for the close.** Each exit fill posts its share to
your account balance as it lands, which is the moment your balance moves.

When your exits balance the entered quantity, Tradr closes the position itself.
**Close Position** stays disabled while any quantity is still open, with the
tooltip *Exit the full quantity first*. The button covers the cases the automatic
path does not.

**Expected result:** the position is tagged **closed** and carries its realized
figures.

<Screenshot name="position-detail" alt="A closed AAPL position, showing average entry and exit prices, gross and net P&L, return percent, notes, and a Fills table with one entry fill and one exit fill." />

The figure above is the sample account's closed AAPL position. It entered 50
shares at 182.40 and exited at 191.20, paying 1.00 in fees each side. Gross P&L
is +$440.00, net P&L is +$438.00, and the return is +4.8%.

Positions also support **scale-ins**, **partial exits**, and **reopening**. See
[Log and manage positions](/user-guide/positions/) for the full lifecycle,
including deleting a draft you decide not to take.

## Step 6 — Read your dashboard

Go back to the **Dashboard**. The widgets now carry your own figures. Six ship by
default: **Stats Summary**, **Performance Chart**, **Equity Curve**, **Account
Balances**, **Position Sizing**, and **Open Positions**.

**Expected result:** all four checklist items are ticked. The checklist then
retires and stops rendering.

The dashboard is a **drag-and-drop widget grid**. Use **Add Widget** to add a
panel and **Reset layout** to start over. Your layout is saved to your account.
See [Customise the dashboard](/user-guide/dashboard/).

## Step 7 — Review your performance

1. Open **Performance**.
2. Choose a timeframe: **Daily**, **Weekly**, **Monthly**, **Yearly**, **YTD**,
   or **All-Time**.

**Expected result:** an equity curve for the window, a **Statistics** panel, and
a breakdown table by period. With one closed position you get a single row. The
picture fills in as you log more.

<Screenshot name="performance" alt="The Performance page, showing an equity curve, a Statistics panel with total positions, net P&L and win rate, and a monthly breakdown table." />

The figure above is the sample account across six months. It shows 10 closed
positions, a total net P&L of +$1,728.00, and a win rate of 70.0%.

## You're done

You have completed a full loop: **account → calculator → draft → open → closed →
dashboard**. From here, keep logging real positions. The value compounds as your
history grows.
