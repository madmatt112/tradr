This is a hand-held first session. By the end you will have created an account,
set up a brokerage account with a starting balance, logged a trade through its
full lifecycle, seen it on your dashboard and equity curve, asked the AI advisor
one question, and reviewed your performance. It takes about ten minutes and
nothing here can go wrong — every step tells you what you should see.

This guide describes the **hosted app at `app.tradr.cloud`**. If you run your own
instance, the screens are identical; the few differences are called out in
**Self-hosted** notes.

<SelfHosted>
  Open your instance at its own address instead (by default
  `http://localhost:8080`). See [Self-host with Docker
  Compose](/self-hosting/docker-compose/) to stand one up first.
</SelfHosted>

## Before you start

You need:

- An email address and a browser (latest Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge).
- Nothing else. Tradr works as a plain manual journal — no broker connection, no
  API keys, no payment required to complete this tutorial.

## Step 1 — Create your account

1. Go to **https://app.tradr.cloud** and choose **Sign up**.
2. Enter your email and a password, then submit.

**Expected result:** you land on an empty dashboard. There are no trades yet, so
most widgets show zeros or an empty state — that is correct.

<HostedOnly>
  You may be asked to verify your email. Click the link in the message to
  confirm; you can still explore before verifying.
</HostedOnly>

<SelfHosted>
  Email verification is only active if the operator configured SMTP. If it is
  off, your account is usable immediately. If you ever forget your password on an
  instance without email, the operator can reset it from the server with `tradr
  reset-password <your-email>`.
</SelfHosted>

## Step 2 — Add a brokerage account with a starting balance

Every trade is booked against an **account** — this is how Tradr tracks your
balance and builds your equity curve. Create one before logging trades.

1. Open **Accounts** from the main navigation.
2. Choose **New account**.
3. Fill in:
   - **Name** — e.g. `Main` or your broker's name.
   - **Currency** — e.g. `USD`. Pick the currency this account trades in.
   - **Starting balance** — e.g. `10000`. This is your opening cash; the equity
     curve begins here.
4. Save.

**Expected result:** the account appears in your accounts list showing a balance
of your starting amount (e.g. `$10,000.00`). This opening balance is the
baseline every future P&L figure is measured against.

Tradr supports **multiple accounts in different currencies** and consolidates
them into a single view. For this tutorial one account is enough — see
[Accounts & the multi-currency ledger](/user-guide/accounts/) when you are
ready to add more.

## Step 3 — Log your first trade through its lifecycle

A Tradr position moves through three states:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | An idea you are planning but have not entered. No effect on your balance. |
| **Open** | You are in the trade. It shows in open positions and uses live/marked P&L. |
| **Closed** | You have exited. Realised P&L is booked to the account. |

You will walk a trade through all three.

### 3a. Create a draft

1. Open **Positions** and choose **New position**.
2. Select the **account** you created in Step 2.
3. Enter the trade idea:
   - **Symbol** — e.g. `AAPL`.
   - **Side** — `Long`.
   - **Quantity** — e.g. `10` shares.
   - **Planned entry price** — e.g. `200`.
   - Optionally a **target**, a **stop**, and a **note** describing the setup.
4. Save it as a **Draft**.

**Expected result:** the position appears in your Positions list tagged
**Draft**. Your account balance is unchanged — a draft is just a plan.

> **Tip:** the [Trade calculator](/user-guide/trade-calculator/) turns an
> entry, target, and stop into a suggested position size, risk/reward ratio, and
> dollar risk. Use it to size the trade before you commit.

### 3b. Open the position

When you actually enter the trade:

1. Open the draft and choose **Open** (or **Add fill**).
2. Confirm the **entry price**, **quantity**, **fill date**, and any **fees**.
3. Submit.

**Expected result:** the position is now tagged **Open** and shows in your open
positions. If you added fees, they are recorded against the trade.

### 3c. Close the position

When you exit:

1. Open the position and choose **Close** (or **Add closing fill**).
2. Enter the **exit price** (e.g. `210`), **quantity**, **date**, and any exit
   **fees**.
3. Submit.

**Expected result:** the position is tagged **Closed** and shows a **realised
P&L** (e.g. a $10 move on 10 shares ≈ `+$100.00`, less fees). Your **account
balance updates** to reflect the gain or loss.

Positions also support **partial closes**, **scale-ins**, and **multiple
fills**. See [Log and manage positions](/user-guide/positions/) for the full
lifecycle, including deleting a draft you decide not to take.

## Step 4 — See it on the dashboard and equity curve

1. Go back to the **Dashboard**.

**Expected result:** the widgets are no longer empty. You should see:

- **Stats** reflecting one closed trade (e.g. trade count, win rate, net P&L).
- The **equity curve** now showing a line that starts at your $10,000 opening
  balance and steps to your post-trade balance.
- Your **account balance** widget showing the updated figure.

The dashboard is a **drag-and-drop widget grid** — add, remove, and rearrange
panels (stats, open positions, performance charts, account balances, position
sizing). Your layout is saved to your account. See [Customise the
dashboard](/user-guide/dashboard/).

## Step 5 — Ask the AI advisor one question

The AI advisor is a conversational assistant that can analyse trade ideas and,
if you opt in, reason over your own trade history.

<HostedOnly>
  The advisor needs either **wallet credits** (buy a credit pack and pay per use
  against your balance) **or** your **own API key** (BYOK — paste an
  Anthropic/OpenAI key in settings and you are billed by the provider directly).
  Pick whichever you prefer in **Settings → Advisor**. Without one of the two, the
  advisor is simply unavailable — nothing breaks.
</HostedOnly>

<SelfHosted>
  There are no wallet credits. You (or your operator) supply an LLM API key,
  either as a platform key in the server's `.env` or as your own BYOK key in
  settings. See [How the AI advisor
  works](/user-guide/explanation/how-the-advisor-works/).
</SelfHosted>

Once a key or credits are in place:

1. Open the **Advisor**.
2. (Optional) turn on **trade-data access** so the advisor can see your positions
   and accounts for personalised answers.
3. Ask something concrete, e.g. _"Review my AAPL trade — was the risk/reward
   reasonable?"_
4. Send.

**Expected result:** the advisor streams back a response token by token. If you
granted data access, it can reference the trade you just logged. You can also
**upload a chart screenshot** for the advisor to read (requires a vision-capable
model).

> Prefer a provider without tool-use? You still get a **conversation-only** mode
> — you just lose live market-data fetching. This is surfaced when you pick the
> model.

## Step 6 — Review your performance

1. Open **Performance**.
2. Choose a timeframe — **daily, weekly, monthly, yearly, YTD,** or **all-time**.

**Expected result:** a P&L breakdown for the chosen period and an equity curve
covering it. With one closed trade you will see a single data point; the picture
fills in as you log more.

## You're done

You have completed a full loop: **account → draft → open → closed → dashboard →
advisor → performance.** From here, keep logging real trades — the value
compounds as your history grows.
